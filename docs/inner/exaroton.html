Exaroton

Minecraft server control as slash commands — with the whitelist attached to the room, not the person.

What it does

/server status|start|stop|restart <server>, registered per guild via command_guilds in exaroton_watch.json, backed by exaroton_ops.py. Per guild: servers (which exaroton servers that guild may see — "*" or a list; the argument only autocompletes to these) and require_operator (if true, start/stop/restart need an owner id or someone with Administrator / Manage Server; status is always open within the whitelist). Current: Testing = all servers, operators required; Chillbar = Isle of Berk only, anyone. A background watchdog posts crash / offline / back-online alerts for servers flagged watch: true.

Why we chose this

Gated per guild, not per user, because the question is "what may this room control", not "who is trusted". Chillbar's residents restarting their own Isle of Berk without pinging you is the feature; the same people can't touch any other server from there because the room's whitelist, not their standing, is what's consulted. It's the same shape as destructive_guilds: place-scoped authority that no amount of asking can widen.

Slash commands, not chat parsing: plain text is never command-triggered — the bot records what it sees and stays quiet. It engages only when addressed: an owner DM, an @-mention, or a guest DM. Two things act without being addressed, and both are timers rather than models: the conversation tick loop, which can only deliver an ask a human already opened, and the watchdog described below, which is the one component here that both speaks and acts on its own.

The watchdog posts outside the chokepoint (straight channel.send) — tolerable where webhook.py is tolerable and for the same reason: a fixed, code-authored message shape ("server X crashed") with no model and no stranger text anywhere in the loop. See Webhook identity for the general rule.

Paper trail

Alerts and command responses are visible in the channels themselves; exaroton_watch.json is the authority record (which guild may touch which server); slash-command sync lines appear in the boot log.
